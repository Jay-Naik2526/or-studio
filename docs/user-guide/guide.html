01 / What OR/Studio is

What OR/Studio is, and is not

A free workbench that solves Operations Research problems and explains every step.

Sheet01 of 25User guide

Operations Research (OR) uses maths to make good decisions: how much to make, where to ship, which job to do first, how many staff to hire. OR/Studio is a website that solves these problems for you, and shows how.

1What it is

  • A solver and a teacher in one. It gives the answer, then lets you move through the working one step at a time, with a short reason for each step.
  • Exact. It keeps fractions as fractions: 1/3 stays 1/3 and is never rounded to 0.333. This makes special cases (such as ties and cycling) easy to detect.
  • Free and private. No account, no server, no payment. Everything runs on your own device. It works offline after the first visit.
  • Made for learning. A step player, a Tutor mode that asks you to choose each move, a Diff mode that finds your mistake, and 66 worked problems.
  • Open source. MIT licence. Anyone can read, copy and improve the code.

2What it is not

  • Not an industrial solver. It is made for textbook-size problems (up to a few hundred variables). Use professional software for very large models.
  • Not a replacement for learning. It shows the method, but you still need to understand the idea. Use it to check your own work.
  • Not always exact. Linear, integer, transport, assignment, network, game and Markov solvers use exact fractions. Queues, inventory and simulation use ordinary decimal numbers, as is normal for these topics.
  • Not connected to the TORA software. It follows the same textbook course (Taha, Operations Research: An Introduction) but is a separate program.

02 / Getting started

Getting started

Open the app, use it offline, keep your work private, and use it on a phone.

Sheet02 of 25User guide

1Open it in any browser

  1. Open a modern browser: Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari or Brave.
  2. Go to or-studio.antideploy.app. The overview page opens.
  3. Click a module card, or press / and type what you want. Try reddy.

There is nothing to install and no sign-in. The first visit needs the internet.

2Use it offline

After your first visit, the app saves itself in the browser. Next time it opens even without the internet. When you are online again, the app checks for a newer version each time you open it. See the FAQ to force an update.

TipBefore an exam or a trip, open the app once while you are online. Then you can use it on a plane or in a lab with no network.

3Install it as an app

OR/Studio is a progressive web app. That means your browser can put it on your desktop or home screen, so it opens in its own window like any other program.

Chrome, Edge, Brave (computer)Click the install icon at the right end of the address bar, or open the browser menu and choose Install OR-Studio.
Android (Chrome)Open the menu and choose Install app or Add to Home screen.
iPhone or iPad (Safari)Tap Share, then Add to Home Screen.

The exact words depend on your browser version. If you do not see an install option, you can keep using the website normally.

4Your work stays on your device

  • OR/Studio has no server. The solving happens inside your browser. Your problems are never sent anywhere.
  • Your work is saved in your browser's own storage on this device only. Clearing the browser's site data removes it.
  • A share link holds the whole model inside the link itself. Anyone who has the link can open your model, so share it only with people you trust.

5Light and dark theme

Use the moon / sun button in the top bar to switch. The app opens in the light theme and remembers your choice. You can also open the search box (/) and run Switch light / dark theme. Printed reports always use a clean light layout.

6Using it on a phone

  • The left rail turns into a menu button (three lines, top right). Tap it to see all modules.
  • On a narrow screen the 01 Model card starts closed to save space. Tap Show to type your problem, and Hide when done.
  • The step player shows Previous / Play / Next and the slider. The first/last and speed buttons are hidden to save space.
  • On a touch screen, tap a short form (such as RHS) to see its meaning.
  • Turn the phone sideways to see wide tables.

You can search for:

The workspace explained

Every module uses the same screen, laid out like a worked sheet. Learn it once and you know all 13.

Sheet04 of 25User guide

Read the screen from top to bottom. First the title block, then 01 Model (what you type), then 02 Solution (the answer and the reasons). The numbers on the picture match the list below it.

  • 1Title block. The module code (LP), its name and the methods inside it.
  • 2Method switch. Choose how the problem is solved (here Auto, Simplex, Two-Phase, Big-M, Dual). Not every module has one.
  • 3Auto / Tutor. Auto solves and shows every step. Tutor hides the next move so you can choose it.
  • 4Toolbar. Export, Save, Open, and Copy share link (left to right).
  • 5Presentation mode (also key P). Bigger text for a classroom screen.
  • 601 Model. Where you enter or load the problem. Anything you change updates the solution at once.
  • 7Hide / Show. Closes or opens the Model card, to give the solution more room.
  • 802 Solution. Everything the solver found.
  • 9Result banner. Status (such as OPTIMAL), the key number, the method, the number of steps and the time.
  • 10Tabs. Different views of the same answer: tables, graphs, sensitivity, and more.
  • 11Explanation. A short sentence for the current step. Open Full reasoning for more.
  • 12Step player. Move through the solution: first, previous, play, next, last, and a slider.
  • 13Speed. How fast Play runs: 0.5×, 1×, 2× or 4×. Hidden on small screens.

4.1The parts in more detail

1Result banner and status colours

The banner is the first thing to read. It uses colour and words, so you never depend on colour alone.

OptimalThe best answer was found. The key number (for example max z = 21) is shown next to it.
Warning kindsSomething needs your attention, for example an alternate optimum, a degenerate answer, or an iteration limit.
Infeasible / UnboundedNo answer exists, or the answer has no limit. Chapter 21 explains what to do.

2Explanation card

It explains the step you are looking at. It has a short sentence (for example x₂ enters, s₂ leaves. Pivot element = 4/3.), a Full reasoning paragraph, a formula where one helps, and a Rule applied line that names the rule. A yellow note appears if something special happened.

3Tabs

Tabs show the same problem in different ways. Their names depend on the module. In Linear programming they are Simplex, Graphical, Sensitivity, Duality, Basic solutions, Primal–dual and Diff mode. Use the mouse, or click a tab and then use ← → to move between tabs.

4Step player

The player is fixed at the bottom of the screen so it is always within reach. See chapter 6. In Tutor mode it is replaced by the Tutor card, because you now decide when to move on.

5Hide and Show the model

Click Hide at the right end of the 01 Model line to close the input and see more of the solution. Click Show to open it again. The app remembers your choice.

6Presentation mode

Press P, or click the last icon in the toolbar. The Model card is hidden, and the writing and tables become larger, so a class at the back of the room can read them. Press P or Esc to leave.

TipTeachers: load a problem from the library, press P, and use → to walk the class through the method one step at a time. Fig 6.4 shows the result.

5.1The LP text format

Write the model the way you would on paper. The objective (what you want to make as large or as small as possible) comes first. The constraints (the limits) follow.

max 3x1 + 5x2
subject to
  labour: x1 + 2x2 <= 10
  3x1 + 2x2 >= 6
  x1 - x2 = 1
  free x3          (x3 may be negative)
  int x1           (x1 must be a whole number)
  bin x2           (x2 must be 0 or 1)
max or minStarts the objective. After it, write the constraints under subject to (you can also write st or s.t.).
<= >= =The relations. You can also type ≤ and ≥. Variables may be on both sides.
x1, x2 >= 0Variables are already non-negative. Writing this line is allowed but not needed.
x1 means 1·x₁Coefficients may be left out. Fractions are fine: 1/2x.
2 <= x + y <= 8A range in one line.
labour:A name for a row. It then appears in the results instead of (1), (2)…
free / int / binDeclarations: unrestricted sign, whole number, or 0/1 (zero or one).

The line below the box tells you if the text was understood: 2 variables, 4 constraints — parsed. If something is wrong, the app shows the line and column and puts a caret under the problem.

Watch outAlways look at the line under the box. If the app cannot read your text, it stops and points at the problem. The old solution stays on screen until the text is valid again. If you are stuck, rewrite the line in its simplest form: coefficient, variable, relation, number.

5.2The Form tab

Prefer a grid? Click Form at the top right of the Model card. It edits the same model. Choose Maximise or Minimise, add or remove variables with − and +, type the numbers, pick the relation (≤, ≥ or =) and the right-hand side. The Type row sets each variable to ≥ 0, free, integer or binary. Switch back to Text at any time. The text is rewritten from the form.

5.3Grids (matrix editors)

Transportation, Assignment, Games, Markov and Job sequencing use a grid. Click a cell and type.

  • Move: Enter or ↓ goes down a cell, ↑ goes up. You can also click any cell.
  • Add or remove a row or column: use the − and + buttons next to Rows and Columns under the grid. A square grid (Markov) has one Size button. The buttons stop at the largest size the module allows.
  • Names: the row and column headings are boxes. Click one and type a new name, such as Plant 1 or Job A.
  • Numbers: whole numbers, decimals (0.75) and fractions (3/4) are accepted. A cell that is not a number is marked as wrong.
  • Prohibited route: in Transportation and Assignment, type M or x in a cell to forbid it.

+Paste from Excel or a CSV file

  1. In your spreadsheet, select the block of numbers and copy it (Ctrl/⌘ + C).
  2. In OR/Studio, click the top-left cell where the block should start.
  3. Paste (Ctrl/⌘ + V). The grid grows if the block is larger, up to the module's limit.

You can also click Import CSV under the grid and choose a .csv, .tsv or .txt file.

TipPaste only the numbers, not the headings. Then rename the rows and columns in the app.

6.2Tutor mode

Switch Auto to Tutor in the title block. The solver now stops before each decision and asks you. For example in the simplex method it asks Which variable should enter the basis? and then Which variable leaves?. Choose an answer.

  • A wrong answer turns red and explains the specific mistake, with a line called Misconception.
  • A right answer turns green. Press Continue for the next question.
  • After two wrong answers a Show the answer button appears.
  • The badge on the card (for example 3/5 first try) counts questions you got right on the first try.

Tutor mode is in Linear programming, Integer programming, Transportation, Assignment, Network models, Project planning, Zero-sum games and Job sequencing. The other modules use formulas and have no decisions to practise. Changing the model or the method restarts the lesson.

6.3Diff mode

Solve the problem by hand first. Then open the Diff mode tab and type your working for one step. OR/Studio compares it with the correct working, finds the first place where they differ, lists the cells, and guesses the cause: a wrong entering or leaving variable, a pivot row that was not divided, a wrong multiplier, a sign error, or a single slip in arithmetic.

  1. Open Diff mode and choose My tableau is for iteration (the step you are checking).
  2. Type your numbers in the grid. Copy solver tableau to edit fills it with the correct one so you can change a cell on purpose to see the message.
  3. Press Compare with iteration, or Find the closest iteration if you are not sure which step you did.

Diff mode is in Linear programming, Integer programming, Transportation, Assignment, Network models, Project planning and Zero-sum games.

6.4The problem library

The library holds 66 worked problems, one or more for every module: textbook examples, special cases (unbounded, infeasible, degenerate) and the course experiments. Open it from the left rail, or search for a problem with /.

  • Each card shows the source, a short description and the expected answer in green.
  • Open loads the problem in its module with the right method chosen.
  • Every answer is checked by the automatic tests of the app, so you can trust it.
  • This guide gives the library id (such as lp-reddy) with each example. A link ending in ?lib=lp-reddy opens it directly.

6.5Presentation mode

See chapter 4.1. Press P to enlarge the screen for a class, and P or Esc to leave.

08 / Linear programming

Linear programming

Simplex · Two-Phase · Big-M · Dual · Graphical · Sensitivity · Duality · Basic solutions · Primal–dual

Sheet08 of 25Optimise

1What it solves

You choose how much of several things to do (the variables) to make one number as big (profit) or as small (cost) as possible (the objective). Your choices are limited by constraints, such as the hours or material you have. In linear programming, everything is a straight-line relation.

2When to use it

  • Product mix: how many of each product to make with limited machines, time or material.
  • Blending and diet: the cheapest mix that meets minimum needs.
  • Any "best use of limited resources" problem where doubling the input doubles the effect.

If answers must be whole numbers, use Integer programming. If the relation is curved, use Nonlinear programming.

3How to enter your problem

  1. Open Linear programming from the rail (code LP).
  2. In 01 Model, type the model (chapter 5.1), use the Form tab, or choose an Example.
  3. Look for parsed in green under the box. If you see red text, fix the line it points to.
  4. Choose a Method in the title block (see the table below). Auto is the right choice if you are not sure.
  5. Use the step player to walk through the simplex tableaux.
MethodWhat it does
AutoUses the plain Simplex if every row is ≤ with a right-hand side of 0 or more. Otherwise it uses Two-Phase.
SimplexThe standard tableau method, starting from the slack variables.
Two-PhasePhase I finds a feasible starting point with artificial variables. Phase II then optimises the real objective.
Big-MArtificial variables with a very large penalty M. The app keeps M as a symbol, never as a number.
DualDual simplex. It needs a start that is optimal but not yet feasible (a minimisation with costs of 0 or more). If it cannot start, the app switches method and tells you why.

4What you get

The banner shows the status and the best value. The tabs are:

SimplexEach tableau, the pivot, the ratio test, and below it the Solution card: variable values, slack of each constraint and its shadow price.
GraphicalOnly for two variables. Draw the constraints and the feasible region and see the best corner. You can drag a constraint's right-hand side.
SensitivityHow far each right-hand side and cost can change before the answer changes. Range bars, a what-if slider and a sentence in plain words.
DualityThe dual problem written out, a table that matches primal and dual parts, and checks (such as strong duality).
Basic solutionsEvery possible basis, with its point and objective value. It marks the feasible ones (the corner points).
Primal–dualThe primal–dual algorithm, with its own step player.
Diff modeCompare your hand-made tableau with the correct one (chapter 6.3).

5How to read the result

  • The z row of the final tableau has no negative number (for a maximisation). That is the sign that the answer is optimal.
  • Basis lists the variables that have a value. Every other variable is 0. RHS gives their values.
  • Slack is the unused part of a resource. A constraint with slack 0 is tight: it is fully used.
  • Shadow price is how much the best value improves if you get one more unit of that resource. A constraint that is not tight has a shadow price of 0.
  • On the Sensitivity tab, the green bar is the range where the shadow price stays valid.
  • On the Duality tab, All checks hold means primal and dual agree.

10 / Nonlinear programming

Nonlinear programming

Gradient and Newton steps · KKT check · exact quadratic programming

Sheet10 of 25Optimise

1What it solves

It finds the lowest (or highest) value of a curved function, such as (x1 − 3)² + 2(x2 + 1)², with or without constraints. It gives three tools, chosen with the Method switch.

2When to use it

  • Unconstrained: find the minimum of a function with an iterative method.
  • KKT: check whether a point satisfies the Karush–Kuhn–Tucker conditions (the standard test for a constrained optimum).
  • Quadratic: solve minimise ½xᵀQx + cᵀx subject to Ax ≤ b, x ≥ 0 exactly.

3How to enter your problem

  1. Open Nonlinear programming and choose Unconstrained, KKT or Quadratic.
  2. Unconstrained: type f(x₁, x₂, …) using x1, x2, the signs + − * / ^ and the functions exp, ln, sin, cos, sqrt. Type a Starting point such as 0, 0. Pick Newton or Gradient descent.
  3. KKT: type the objective, add constraints with + constraint (each is g(x) ≤ 0 or h(x) = 0), and type the Candidate point.
  4. Quadratic: fill the symmetric matrix Q, the vector c, and the constraint grid A | b.

4What you get

  • Unconstrained: a contour plot with the path of the iterates, symbolic derivatives (the gradient and the Hessian), and a step player.
  • KKT: a list of the four conditions, each marked satisfied or not, with the multipliers λ.
  • Quadratic: the exact optimum, and whether the problem is convex.

5How to read the result

  • Stationary point — minimum: the gradient is almost zero and the Hessian eigenvalues are all positive. It is a local minimum, and the global one if the function is convex.
  • Contours are lines of equal f. The path should move across them towards the centre.
  • KKT satisfied means the point is a candidate optimum. It is sufficient only if the problem is convex.

6Worked mini example

Watch out
  • Write the product with a star: 2*x1. Use ^ for powers: x1^2.

  • Irreducible: every state can be reached from every other. Aperiodic: the chain does not run in a fixed cycle.
  • Steady state π is the share of time spent in each state in the long run. It solves πP = π with the shares adding up to 1.
  • Mean first-passage time is the average number of steps to reach a state for the first time. On the diagonal it is the mean return time, 1/πⱼ.

6Worked mini example

Library problem mk-weather: P = [[0.7, 0.3], [0.4, 0.6]], start Sunny, n = 10. The chain is irreducible and aperiodic. The steady state is π = (4/7, 3/7), about 0.571 sunny and 0.429 rainy. After 10 steps from Sunny, the distribution is already 0.5714 and 0.4286. The mean return time to Sunny is 7/4 steps.

Try mk-abs (gambler's ruin, with absorbing states Broke and Win) and mk-period (a flip-flop of period 2: Pⁿ never settles, yet π = (½, ½) exists).

7Tips and common mistakes

Watch out
  • Check the Σ column: every row must add up to 1.
  • A periodic chain has no limiting distribution for Pⁿ, but it still has a steady state.

MethodUse it for

MessageWhat it meansWhat to do
InfeasibleNo answer can satisfy every constraint at once. The app shows which constraints conflict, and a combination of them that gives a false statement (such as 0 ≥ 3). Troubleshooting and FAQ

Quick answers when something looks wrong.

Sheet23 of 25Reference

The page says "Something went wrong in this view"

The app shows this message, and the reason, if a view fails. Try these in order: press Try again; if the same message returns, press Reset this module. A different message, This screen could not be downloaded, means the connection dropped or the app was updated while the page was open. Press Reload. Your work is autosaved.

My numbers look different from my textbook

Check these causes before you suspect a mistake. The final answer should agree; the path may not.

  • Different choice at a tie. Two variables may tie to enter or leave. Books choose one; the app follows a fixed rule and says when there was a tie. This can give a different pivot order, or a different (equally good) optimal corner.
  • Alternate optima. The best value is the same, but the point may differ (chapter 21).
  • Different entering rule. The app picks the most negative reduced cost (Dantzig's rule). Some books use other rules.
  • Minimisation shown as maximisation. For a minimisation, the tableau may show z (shown as max −z). The sign of the z row is then reversed.
  • Rounding. The app keeps exact fractions (3/2), while books often round (1.5). Check the decimal value next to the fraction (for example ≈1.5).
  • Starting methods. NW corner, least cost and Vogel give different starts but the same final cost.
  • Random numbers. A simulation depends on the seed or the random numbers you typed. Use Given list to reproduce a textbook table exactly.
  • Interval convention. 00–99 versus 01–100 in simulation tables (see walk-through B).

Use Diff mode to find where your hand working and the solver first differ.
